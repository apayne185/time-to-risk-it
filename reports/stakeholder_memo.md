# Memo: an early-warning list for responsible-gambling outreach

**To:** Head of Responsible Gambling · **Re:** whether to pilot a monthly risk list ·
**Evidence:** bwin player histories 2005–2010 (research data), tested on Aug–Oct 2009

## The decision

Each month your team can proactively contact only a small share of active players. Today the
choice of whom to contact relies on rules of thumb. The question is whether a model-ranked list
reaches more of the players who will need an intervention soon, and what that costs in contacts.

## What the evidence shows

Ranking players by the model's 30-day risk and contacting the top of the list each month:

| Monthly budget (share of active players) | Upcoming cases reached: model | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| 1% | 11% | 7% |
| 5% | **43%** | 22% |
| 10% | **62%** | 36% |

"Upcoming cases" are players who went on to trigger an RG intervention within the next 30 days.
**At a 5–10% budget the model reaches roughly twice as many of them as the rule.**

Every flagged player comes with the three behaviours that raised their score, described in plain
language, for example *"share of the last 30 days with a bet"* or *"largest single-day stake,
last 365 days"*, with the player's own value. Agents can open the conversation with something the
player recognises in their own activity.

## The trade-off you are choosing

- **Most contacts will be with players who would not have needed one.** Depending on how common
  RG cases really are among active players, it takes roughly 12 to 85 contacts to reach one player
  before their intervention. This is why the list should drive **supportive** outreach (tools,
  information, a conversation), never restrictions.
- **Some players will be missed.** Even at a 10% budget, about 4 in 10 upcoming cases are not on
  the list, and players who have not bet in the last 90 days are never scored.
- **Bigger budgets reach more people, with diminishing returns.** The step from 5% to 10% adds
  about 19 points of coverage for twice the effort.

## What it needs to stay reliable

1. **A monthly check.** The share of players needing intervention changed during 2009. A monthly
   job compares last month's predictions with what happened and adjusts automatically.
2. **Feedback from your team.** Whether each contacted player engaged, set limits or later needed
   an intervention, so effectiveness can be measured, not assumed.
3. **Group-level fairness checks.** The list ranks men and women, and all age groups, comparably
   well, but it understates absolute risk for women and players over 35. Monitoring should track
   this every month.

## Recommendation

Run a **three-month pilot at a 5% monthly budget**, alongside the current process, with a random
hold-out group so the effect of outreach itself can be measured. Decide on wider use from the
pilot's results, after revalidating the model on current customers: this evidence is from
2005–2010 and one operator.
