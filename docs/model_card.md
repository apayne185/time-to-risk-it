# Model card: 30-day responsible-gambling risk (XGBoost Cox)

Format after Mitchell et al. (2019), *Model Cards for Model Reporting*. Numbers are from
`reports/evaluation.md` and `reports/models_primary.md`, regenerated by `make evaluate`.

## Model details

| | |
|---|---|
| **Model** | Gradient-boosted Cox proportional-hazards model (XGBoost `survival:cox`, depth 2, min child weight 50), Breslow baseline hazard |
| **Output** | Risk score (ranking), calibrated probability of a harm-onset RG intervention within 30 days, contact flag under a monthly capacity policy, top 3 drivers |
| **Calibration** | Weighted Platt recalibration on out-of-fold predictions, plus a monthly intercept update from the latest month with complete outcomes |
| **Inputs** | 47 behavioural features from daily betting aggregates before the scoring date ([docs/features.md](features.md)). No demographics. |
| **Selection** | Chosen among Cox, XGBoost Cox and XGBoost AFT by out-of-fold within-month AUC, before test evaluation |
| **Artifact** | `models/primary/decision_model.joblib` (model, recalibration, policy threshold, git SHA, creation time) |
| **Version** | 0.1.0, trained 2026-10-05 |

## Intended use

- **Primary use:** rank active players monthly so a responsible-gambling team can prioritise
  **human review and supportive outreach** (information, limit-setting tools, a conversation)
  within a fixed monthly capacity.
- **Users:** RG analysts and customer-service agents, who see each score with its drivers.
- **Decision support only:** a flag is a prompt to look, not a finding about the player.

## Out-of-scope uses

- Restricting, closing or penalising accounts automatically.
- Marketing, bonus targeting or any use that encourages more play from flagged players.
- Credit, insurance, employment or any decision outside responsible gambling.
- Scoring players who have not bet in the previous 90 days (the model has never seen them).
- Deploying on another operator's or a recent market's players without revalidation (see Caveats).

## Training and evaluation data

bwin daily betting aggregates, 2000–2010, for 4,134 players: 2,068 who triggered an RG
intervention between Nov 2008 and Nov 2009 and 2,066 matched controls (same first-deposit date).
Licensed from The Transparency Project; not redistributed.

- **Outcome (primary label):** first RG event of a harm-onset type (third-party block, account
  closure, cancelled withdrawal, limit change, payment block, partial block, problem reported).
  Accounts that were already closed or self-excluded before the window are excluded
  ([ADR 0002](decisions/0002-primary-label.md)).
- **Design:** monthly landmarks Nov 2008–Oct 2009; eligible players had bet in the prior 90 days;
  10,378 player-months, 1,885 players ([ADR 0003](decisions/0003-landmark-design.md)).
- **Splits by time:** train Nov 2008–Apr 2009 (6,106 rows), validation May–Jul 2009 (2,458),
  test Aug–Oct 2009 (1,814 rows, 237 events within 30 days). Outcomes are censored at split
  boundaries.
- **Population scale:** case-control weights assume 0.5% of active players become harm-onset
  cases over 13 months; results are reported for 0.25%–2%
  ([ADR 0005](decisions/0005-decision-layer.md)).

## Metrics

Ranking is measured **within each scoring month** (pooled metrics would reward the sampling
design). Capacity metrics contact the top share of active players each month.

## Quantitative analysis by subgroup

Test split, with the monthly update. Demographics are not inputs; 167 controls with missing
demographics are excluded. Groups with < 20 events are noisy.

| Group | Rows | Events | AUC | O/E |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Men | 1,636 | 208 | 0.77 | 1.05 |
| Women | 178 | 29 | 0.88 | 2.10 |
| Age 18–24 | 403 | 47 | 0.83 | 1.02 |
| Age 25–34 | 800 | 103 | 0.76 | 0.89 |
| Age 35–49 | 426 | 71 | 0.75 | 1.63 |
| Age 50+ | 110 | 16 | 0.82 | 3.47 |
| Germany | 689 | 106 | 0.78 | 1.81 |
| Other countries (incl. France, Poland, Spain) | 1,050 | 131 | 0.72–0.79 | 0.78–1.17 |

**Ranking quality is comparable or better across groups. Risk is under-predicted for women,
players aged 35+, and German players** (O/E > 1.5): the model ranks them sensibly but understates
their absolute risk. Under a capacity policy this means they are contacted somewhat less often
than their true risk warrants. Mitigation: monitor per-group O/E monthly and recalibrate per group
if the gap persists; do not add demographic features.

## What the model relies on

Largest mean contributions on test: days since last bet, largest single-day stake (1-year,
3-month and 1-month windows), number of product types mixed in one day, stake volatility,
tenure, and recent change in betting frequency ([figure](../reports/figures/drivers.png)).
These are consistent with behavioural markers reported in the Internet-gambling literature
(intensity, escalation, product variety) and with the replicated finding that frequent
live-action betting is the strongest single signal.

## Ethical considerations

- **Label is an operator action, not diagnosed harm.** The model partly learns what the
  operator's RG team noticed, and inherits its blind spots. Players who were harmed but never
  triggered an intervention count as controls.
- **False positives** mean unnecessary contacts. With supportive outreach the cost is small but
  real (annoyance, perceived surveillance); with restrictive actions it would not be acceptable,
  hence the out-of-scope list.
- **False negatives** are players who are not reached. At a 10% budget about 38% of next-month
  cases are missed, and inactive players are never scored (205 of 1,034 harm-onset cases).
- **Transparency to the player:** each contact can be explained with the score's drivers, which
  are all behaviours the player can see in their own history.
- **Privacy:** the model uses betting aggregates only; no demographic, payment or device data.

## Caveats and recommendations

- **Age of data:** 2005–2010, one operator, mostly German-speaking markets; products (in-play
  betting, mobile, casino) and regulation have changed. Revalidate before any use on current data.
- **Calibration drift is expected:** the intervention rate rose through 2009. Run `ttr monitor`
  monthly and apply the intercept update; retrain if feature or score PSI exceeds 0.25.
- **Absolute probabilities depend on an assumed population rate.** Prefer capacity-based policies
  (top *k*% per month) over probability thresholds until the true rate is known.
- **Calibration slope 0.70:** risks are too spread out; probability-threshold policies would
  over-contact at the top. Capacity policies are unaffected.
- **Model choice:** the learned models are statistically indistinguishable on ranking (overlapping
  CIs). XGBoost AFT did slightly better on test at small budgets; it was not chosen because
  selection was fixed before the test evaluation.
